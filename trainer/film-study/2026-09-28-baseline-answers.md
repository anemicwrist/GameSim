## 1. T17: a burned Mega Golisopod against a Haze Toxapex

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2683599297-t17.md`

**What mk7juju did [log]:** Swords Dance (+2).

**What happened [log]:** Toxapex used Infestation, which traps Golisopod and chips it every turn. Burn plus Infestation
took Golisopod from 79% to 59%. On T18 a +2 Drill Run took Toxapex to 31%, and Toxapex Hazed again. On T19 Golisopod
used Swords Dance again and Toxapex Hazed again. Golisopod stayed trapped until Toxapex switched out on T20, by which
point it was at 17%. It later KO'd Sableye (T23), fell to Samurott-Hisui (T24), and Gholdengo lost to Samurott-H too
(T25–T26).

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Swords Dance into a revealed Haze is a wasted turn.** Toxapex erases the boost before you can use it, and the
  burn ticks either way.
- **Golisopod can't break Toxapex now.** Burned, unboosted and hitting into Reflect, Drill Run does about 14% (T16).
  Recover heals 50%, and Regenerator heals a third on every switch-out. Every turn Golisopod stays in costs it 6% for
  nothing.
- **Better: switch to Gholdengo.** Burn only damages a Pokémon while it's on the field, so switching stops the clock.
  Gholdengo is immune to Poison-type attacks (Steel) and to status moves such as Toxic and Will-O-Wisp (Good as Gold).
  Save Golisopod's HP for Samurott-Hisui, since Leech Life is 2x on its Dark typing. This was also the **last turn
  Golisopod could leave**: the Infestation used this turn trapped it from T18 until Toxapex switched out.
- **If you stay in anyway,** attack with Drill Run rather than boost. It at least forces Toxapex to spend turns
  Recovering.

**Key idea:** once the opponent reveals Haze, stop setting up. A burned physical attacker's job is chip damage or one
specific target, not a sweep.

## 2. T6: Mega Blaziken against a fresh Mimikyu

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2685829243-t6.md`

**What qhzht did [log]:** switched Blaziken out to Baxcalibur.

**What happened [log]:** Mimikyu used Play Rough (2x on Dragon/Ice) and KO'd Baxcalibur from 100%; Life Orb took
Mimikyu to 78%. Blaziken came back in at 91%. On T7 its Flare Blitz only broke the Disguise (Mimikyu 65%, and no recoil
because the Disguise took no damage), then Play Rough took Blaziken to 22%. On T8 Shadow Sneak (priority) KO'd Blaziken.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Mimikyu's STAB types are Ghost and Fairy, and Baxcalibur is weak to Fairy.** Switching it in handed Mimikyu a free
  KO. A switch into a super-effective STAB only works if you're predicting a different move, and a fresh Mimikyu with
  its Disguise up has no reason to do anything but attack.
- **Stay in and attack instead.** Blaziken is faster. Either attack breaks the Disguise, and Flare Blitz takes no
  recoil when the Disguise absorbs it. Mimikyu's best reply is Play Rough (about 69%, as on T7), and Shadow Sneak can
  then finish Blaziken. So qhzht was probably losing either way at one against two, facing priority. But staying in
  makes LGPESWEEPER find the right sequence, and it doesn't throw away a Pokémon.
- **The Team Preview pick set this up.** Baxcalibur threatens Salamence and Meowscarada on LGPESWEEPER's 6, but it's
  weak to both of Lucario's STAB types (Steel and Fighting) and to Mimikyu's Fairy. Against the three that actually
  came, it had no good matchup. Bringing it was a bet on which three LGPESWEEPER would pick.

**Key idea:** know the opponent's STAB types before you switch, and even in a losing position make them earn it.

## 3. T2: Calm Mind Primarina against a Grimmsnarl lead

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2682454574-t2.md`

**What hengcaiji did [log]:** Calm Mind again.

**What happened [log]:** Grimmsnarl moved first with **Spirit Break** (a physical Fairy move that always lowers the
target's SpA by 1): Primarina 62%, SpA back to +0. Calm Mind then put it at +1 SpA / +2 SpD. On T3 Primarina moved
first and Moonblasted at +1 (Grimmsnarl → 32%), and Spirit Break took Primarina to 27% and +0 SpA. On T4 Grimmsnarl
used Parting Shot into Gholdengo, which swept all three of hengcaiji's Pokémon.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **The Calm Mind plan can't win here.** Grimmsnarl's usual attack, Spirit Break, lowers SpA on every hit (100% chance
  per Showdown move data). Each hit cancels one Calm Mind and takes about 35–38%, as it did on T2 and T3. The +SpD
  boosts do nothing against a physical attack.
- **Light Screen already halves your special damage,** for 5 turns or 8 with Light Clay (it turned out to be 8). Every
  turn you spend boosting is a turn of screen for whatever comes in next.
- **Moonblast now is better.** At +1 SpA it does about 68% through Light Screen: on T3, under the same conditions, it
  did exactly that. Grimmsnarl then has to pivot out or go down a turn earlier, and Primarina is still healthy when it
  happens.
- **Know what the lead is for.** Grimmsnarl's job is to set screens and pivot into a sweeper with Parting Shot. Punish
  it with damage while it's on the field, and don't try to out-boost it.

**Key idea:** against a screens-and-pivot lead, damage now beats setup.

## 4. T4: Grimmsnarl at 32% against Primarina at 27%

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2682454574-t4.md`

**What Gosaimasu did [log]:** Parting Shot.

**What happened [log]:** Parting Shot went first. Primarina dropped to −1 Atk and −1 SpA, and Gholdengo came in and
took a resisted Moonblast (100% → 91%, then 96% after Leftovers). On T5 Gholdengo used Nasty Plot, and on T6 a +2
Shadow Ball KO'd Primarina. Gholdengo then beat Mega Greninja (T7) and Corviknight (T10) to win. Grimmsnarl finished
the game at 32%.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Spirit Break is a coin flip.** It KOs the 27% Primarina only if you win the speed tie. If you lose the tie,
  Primarina's Moonblast (about 45% at +0 through Light Screen) KOs Grimmsnarl first.
- **Parting Shot has no coin flip.** With Prankster it goes first for certain. It cuts Primarina to −1 SpA and brings
  Gholdengo in on a Fairy move that Steel resists. Gholdengo then sets up behind five more turns of Light Screen
  against a Pokémon that can barely scratch it, and Grimmsnarl survives for a second round of screens if needed.
- **It works against this target specifically.** Prankster status moves fail against Dark types, and Primarina isn't
  Dark.

**Key idea:** when your active Pokémon's job is done, turn it into a free switch for your win condition instead of
flipping a coin with it.

## 5. T9: a +4 Samurott-Hisui at 34% against a fresh Golisopod

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2683599297-t9.md`

**What Dragalge THL did [log]:** switched Samurott-Hisui out to Toxapex, giving up the +4.

**What happened [log]:** Golisopod Mega Evolved and used Leech Life, which Toxapex resisted (→ 80%). Samurott-Hisui
stayed out from T9 to T23. It came back at 34%, KO'd Golisopod on T24, and KO'd Gholdengo with two Ceaseless Edges on
T25–T26 to win.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Staying in is a bad trade.** Samurott-H is faster than Golisopod (base Speed 85 against 40) and has +4, but
  Golisopod Mega Evolves before moves resolve and becomes very physically bulky (base Defense 140 → 175). A neutral +4
  Ceaseless Edge isn't a sure OHKO (no calc done). Any Bug move back is 2x on Samurott-H's Dark typing and KOs it at
  34%, and First Impression (+2 priority on Golisopod's first turn in) could KO it before it even moves.
- **Samurott-Hisui is your answer to Gholdengo.** Its Dark STAB is 2x on Gholdengo, and both of Gholdengo's usual STAB
  types are resisted by Samurott-H (Steel by Water, Ghost by Dark). Toxapex (Haze) and Sableye can stall Gholdengo's
  Nasty Plots, but they can't KO it. Losing Samurott-H here for some damage on Golisopod would leave you with no way
  to beat Gholdengo.
- **Toxapex is the right switch-in.** It resists Golisopod's Bug and Water STABs, Regenerator gives back 1/3 when it
  leaves, and Haze answers Swords Dance. Sableye is the other option, since it can burn Golisopod with Will-O-Wisp,
  but it takes Golisopod's hits at neutral.
- The +4 was already worth a KO (Grimmsnarl). Boosts are temporary, and the Pokémon is what matters.

**Key idea:** preserve the Pokémon that beats their remaining win condition, even when that means throwing away boosts.

## 6. T4: a 45% Mega Lucario Z against a fresh Blaziken

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2685829243-t4.md`

**What LGPESWEEPER did [log]:** switched Lucario out to Gyarados.

**What happened [log]:** Gyarados took Stealth Rock (→ 75%) and Intimidated Blaziken (−1 Atk). Blaziken Mega Evolved
and used Flare Blitz, which Gyarados resisted (→ 53%, then 59% after Leftovers), and Speed Boost raised its Speed by 1.
On T5 Mega Blaziken moved first with **Thunder Punch** (4x) and KO'd Gyarados before it acted. LGPESWEEPER still won,
after qhzht switched Baxcalibur into Mimikyu on T6.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Keep Lucario in:** Vacuum Wave (priority, neutral on Fire/Fighting) chips Blaziken first, then Mega Blaziken's
  Flare Blitz (2x on Steel) KOs Lucario. You lose a 45% Pokémon with −1 Speed that can't beat Blaziken anyway, because
  Blaziken resists Steel.
- **Switch to Gyarados (the actual play):** Gyarados resists Fire and Fighting and brings Intimidate. But it pays 25%
  to Stealth Rock just to come in, and after one Speed Boost Blaziken outspeeds it (base 100 at +1 against 81). If
  Blaziken carries Electric (4x) or Rock (2x) coverage, Gyarados never gets a turn. The switch is a bet on Blaziken's
  coverage move, and this Blaziken had Thunder Punch.
- **Switch to Mimikyu:** Disguise absorbs one hit, but Mimikyu loses 1/8 to Stealth Rock and 1/8 when the Disguise
  breaks, and a +1 Blaziken then outspeeds it. That spends your late-game cleaner early.
- **My pick is to let Lucario take the hit.** It spends your least valuable piece and keeps Gyarados and Mimikyu at
  full HP. Blaziken then has to get through Intimidate and a Disguise, with Mimikyu's Shadow Sneak waiting. The Gyarados
  switch is only right if you're confident Blaziken has no Electric or Rock move.

**Key idea:** when your active Pokémon is going to fall anyway, letting it fall can beat a switch that pays hazard
damage and still loses to one coverage move.

## 7. T8: Mega Golisopod against a Libero Cinderace

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2688548425-t8.md`

**What sky2132 did [log]:** Sucker Punch. Their turn timer dropped to 30 seconds on this decision.

**What happened [log]:** Sucker Punch went first but was **resisted** by the Fighting-type Cinderace: 33% → 19%.
Cinderace's Pyro Ball (4x on Bug/Steel) KO'd Golisopod, and Life Orb took Cinderace to 9%. Archaludon came in at 83%
and KO'd Cinderace with Electro Shot on T9. Then Samurott-Hisui came in and won the endgame: Focus Sash held at 1% (T10),
Sacred Sword (2x) left Archaludon at 10%, and Aqua Jet finished it (T11).

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Sucker Punch couldn't work.** Libero changes type only once per switch-in (Showdown ability text: "Once per
  switch-in"), so after High Jump Kick Cinderace stayed Fighting-type, and Fighting resists Dark. A resisted 70-BP hit
  through Aurora Veil does about 14% (it did), against 33% HP. Drill Run was neutral and did 32% last turn, but
  Golisopod is far slower, so Pyro Ball lands first. Staying in means losing Golisopod for about 14% damage.
- **Switch to Archaludon instead.** Cinderace's obvious click into a 45% Mega Golisopod is Pyro Ball, the 4x move.
  Into Archaludon, Pyro Ball is neutral (Steel/Dragon), halved by rain, and **not STAB**, because Cinderace is locked
  Fighting-type. Life Orb then drops Cinderace to about 23%, and Electro Shot (no charge turn in rain) finishes it next
  turn. The risk is a High Jump Kick (2x on Archaludon), but that's the unlikely click against a Bug/Steel target.
- **Why keeping Golisopod matters:** Ghostofcolress's last Pokémon was a full-HP Samurott-Hisui, which turned out to
  hold Focus Sash and Aqua Jet. Against Sash plus priority you want **two hits from two sources**. In the game,
  Archaludon broke the Sash and was left at 10%, with nothing behind it, to die to Aqua Jet. With Golisopod still
  alive (about 28% after Spikes), the 1% Samurott would have faced a second attacker:
  - Sucker Punch beats Samurott's non-priority attacks (Ceaseless Edge, Sacred Sword), because it goes first.
  - A normal attack beats Aqua Jet, because Mega Golisopod can take a weak Aqua Jet and then hit.
  - The catch is that Sucker Punch fails if Samurott's faster Aqua Jet lands first.

  That's a guessing game Ghostofcolress could still win, but it's better than the certain loss that followed T8.

**Key idea:** read the type indicator after Libero or Protean, and when a full-HP Sash-plus-priority Pokémon is still
in the back, don't trade away a Pokémon for 14% damage.

## 8. T7: Mega Gyarados against a +1 Mega Charizard X at 28%

Source position: `knowledge/film/positions/ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2688811994-t7.md`

**What Ch3t1x did [log]:** switched Mega Gyarados out to Garchomp.

**What happened [log]:** Garchomp came in at 83%. Charizard used Thunder Punch, and Garchomp was immune. On T8
Charizard moved first and KO'd Garchomp with Dragon Claw (2x), but Rough Skin took Charizard to 16% and Rocky Helmet
took it to 0. Both fainted, and Ch3t1x won with Mega Gyarados still at 59%.

**Analysis (my read):**
- **Staying in bets the game on a speed stat you haven't seen.** At equal +1, Charizard's base 100 beats Gyarados' 81
  unless your Speed investment is far higher. Its obvious click is Thunder Punch (2x on Water/Dark Mega Gyarados),
  boosted by +1 and Tough Claws, which is a real KO risk at 59%. If Gyarados did move first, Waterfall would KO (it did
  72% last turn against 28% now).
- **The Garchomp switch wins against almost everything:**
  - Thunder Punch does nothing, because Garchomp is Ground-type.
  - Every **contact** move into Garchomp costs Charizard 1/8 from Rough Skin plus 1/6 from Rocky Helmet, which is
    **29.2%, more than the 28% it has left**. Dragon Claw, Flare Blitz and Thunder Punch are all contact moves
    (Showdown move data). So Charizard can KO Garchomp and still die, leaving Gyarados as your last Pokémon standing.
  - If Charizard uses Dragon Dance again, Garchomp gets a free hit on a 28% target.
- **The only escape for Charizard** is a non-contact attack strong enough to KO Garchomp from 83%. None appeared in this
  game, and every move it did use (Dragon Dance, Thunder Punch, Dragon Claw) fits a contact-heavy Tough Claws set.

**Key idea:** count passive damage. Rough Skin (or Iron Barbs) plus Rocky Helmet is about 29% per contact hit, which is
a free KO on a low-HP physical attacker, and the switch-in can win without ever moving.
