## 1. Economic drivers (airport, Aerotropolis, logistics, Gillem, Southlake, employers, data centers)

### Takeaway
Clayton's economy is anchored by Hartsfield-Jackson (about 55,300 on-site jobs; 106.3M passengers in 2025) and an airport-adjacent logistics/data-center build-out (Gillem Logistics Center leasing, a $3B Digital Realty campus and a $959M TA Realty campus approved in 2025), but 2025–2026 also brought a county data-center moratorium and the withdrawal of a 1.25 GW Lovejoy campus, so growth is real yet politically contested and skews toward low-headcount industrial uses rather than high-wage office employment.

### Inferences
- The pipeline is heavily industrial/data-center. Those uses raise the commercial tax digest (helpful for millage pressure) but add few resident jobs per acre (Digital Realty: 60–100 permanent jobs on $3B), so they do little to lift household incomes near Jonesboro. Housing demand in 30238 will continue to be driven by airport/logistics wage earners and by affordability spillover from Fulton/Henry rather than by a new high-income employer base.
- The withdrawal of the Lovejoy campus in favor of 400+ acres of residential development (5–6 miles south of the subject) signals more single-family supply coming to south Clayton, a mild headwind to appreciation for existing older homes.

## 2. Transit and infrastructure (MARTA Clayton BRT, rail, GDOT, Jonesboro LCI, SPLOST, parks)

### Takeaway
Transit is coming but slowly: the Southlake BRT (Rapid B-Line) has slipped from a 2026 opening to construction starting in early 2029 and still lacks guaranteed federal money; the Rapid SR 54 BRT that would actually serve Jonesboro/Lovejoy is only in planning with no LPA; the nearest funded item is the Justice Center Transit Hub on Tara Blvd (construction bids targeted Q3 2026). Commuter rail to Jonesboro was set aside in 2021–2022 in favor of BRT.

## 3. Governance and fiscal health (county and city)

### Takeaway
Clayton County carries investment-grade ratings (Aa2 Moody's / AA S&P per its FY2025 budget book, last reaffirmation cited 2014) and a $522.1M FY2027 budget, but the picture is one of rising taxes and political friction: the net M&O millage jumped from 12.360 (FY2024) to 15.266 (FY2025) and is proposed at 15.052 mills for 2026 (6.44% above rollback), the General Fund balance fell from $115.2M (FY2023) to about $91.7M (FY2024 est.), the 2025 budget fight cut 39 jail officers and drew a lawsuit threat from the sheriff, and the City of Jonesboro censured its mayor in Sept 2026 while raising its own millage from 9.0 to 12.5 mills.

### Inferences
- For a buy-and-hold investor the county's fundamentals support rental demand (airport/logistics wage base, affordability, tenant pool) more than they support owner-occupant resale appreciation; the realistic 5–15-year thesis is cash-flow plus metro-inflation-level appreciation, with the Tara Blvd transit hub and SR 54 BRT as free options rather than underwritable catalysts.
- Rising non-homestead property taxes are the most predictable erosion of investor cash flow over the next five years.
