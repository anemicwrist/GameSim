## Key question 4: Clayton County specifics — foreclosure/REO, investor share, population, income, affordability, and 2020-2026 appreciation vs. Fulton, DeKalb, Henry, Fayette

### Takeaway
Clayton County out-boomed every neighbor in 2020-2022 (FHFA +53% vs. Fulton +31%, DeKalb +29%, Henry +44%, Fayette +35%) and is now the only one giving ground (2023-2025 -0.2% vs. +4% to +7% elsewhere; Zillow -12.4% from peak vs. -0.7% Fayette, -6.0% Fulton, -7.0% Henry). Fundamentals explain the divergence: Clayton's population has been flat-to-down since 2020 (-0.65% to 297,471 in 2025, while Henry grew +10.1%), median household income is the lowest in the core metro ($59,825 in 2024, 59% of Fulton's), roughly a third of its single-family homes are rentals, and Georgia foreclosure starts are up sharply in 2026 (+52% y/y filings in H1 2026). Affordability is nonetheless better than the metro: price-to-income ~3.8x and a $200,000 purchase with 20% down costs about 27% of the county median income at 7.4%.

- Clayton's 2020-22 outperformance was the "cheapest county in the metro" catch-up trade (investor and FHA-buyer demand chasing low entry prices); with that demand cohort now facing 7%+ rates and high investor exposure, the county is retracing first and furthest. - Flat population plus the highest rental share means local owner-occupant demand is thin; exit liquidity for a resale will depend heavily on FHA/first-time buyers and investors, both rate-sensitive groups.
- Affordability is the county's main support: at ~3.8x income and ~27% of median income for a $200k purchase, Clayton is one of the few metro submarkets where a median household can still buy, which limits how far prices can fall if rates ease.
