## Key Question 2: Does the Clayton County assessor reset the 2027 fair market value to the purchase price?

### Takeaway
Clayton's Board of Assessors applies the state rule as a cap, not an automatic reset: "Any 'arms length bona fide sale' of real property shall be the maximum allowable fair market value for a period of one year following the transaction." Because the 2026 notice value ($200,500) already equals the $200,000 list price, a purchase at list leaves 2027 taxes essentially unchanged; a purchase below $200,500 entitles the buyer to a 2027 FMV no higher than the price, but only if the buyer checks the May/June 2027 notice and appeals within 45 days if it comes in higher.

### Cited Findings
- Clayton County Tax Assessor, "Notice of Assessment Information" page (the claytoncountyga.gov/?p=28706 link redirects here): "Any 'arms length bona fide sale' of real property shall be the maximum allowable fair market value for a period of one year following the transaction." Also: "All Georgia real property taxpayers will receive a standardized annual assessment notice. The notice will include an estimate of your ad valorem taxes based on the previous year's millage rate and the current year's fair market value ... All counties must allow taxpayers 45 days from the date of the assessment notice to file an appeal. The appeal must be made in writing" — [Clayton County Tax Assessor, Notice of Assessment Information](https://www.claytoncountyga.gov/government/tax-assessor/notice-of-assessment-information/)
- Statutory basis, O.C.G.A. § 48-5-2(3): the transaction amount of the most recent arm's-length, bona fide sale is the maximum allowable fair market value for the next taxable year, and the definition of arm's-length sale expressly includes distress sales, bank sales and auctions (already in the operating-cost notes) — [Georgia Code § 48-5-2](https://law.onecle.com/georgia/title-48/48-5-2.html)
- Timing of notices and appeals: a 2025 county podcast episode tied the annual assessment-notice appeal deadline to July 14, 2025 — [Clayton Connected Podcast, Episode 16 (2025)](https://claytonconnectedpodcast.claytoncountyga.gov/episode-16-clayton-connected-podcast-annual-assessment-of-notice-deadline-july-14-2025/); Ownwell (a commercial appeal service) lists Clayton notices as expected around May 30 and the appeal deadline around July 14 — [Ownwell Clayton County guide](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/clayton-county-property-tax)
- Georgia's 2026 HOME Act (SB 33) makes the inflation cap on assessment growth mandatory statewide from 2027 for homestead property only; "rental and investment properties are not protected" (already in the operating-cost notes) — [GMA summary of SB 33](https://www.gacities.com/articles/sb-33-the-homeownership-opportunity-and-market-equalization-act-of-2026)

### Inferences
- The rule works in only one direction for this buyer: the sale price caps 2027 FMV; nothing prevents the assessor from leaving 2027 FMV at or below the price. With 2026 FMV already at $200,500, a $200,000 purchase makes the 2027 cap $200,000 (tax about $3,199 at 2026 rates); a $175,000 purchase caps 2027 FMV at $175,000 (about $2,804). The cap protects only tax year 2027; from 2028 the assessor can re-trend the value with the neighborhood, and as a non-homestead owner the buyer gets no HOME Act inflation cap.
- Action for the buyer: watch for the 2027 assessment notice (expected late May/June 2027); if the FMV on it exceeds the recorded purchase price, file a written appeal within 45 days citing § 48-5-2(3) and the closing statement. If the sale is an REO or estate sale, it still counts as arm's-length under the statute.

The buyer can ask the Clayton County Board of Assessors office in Jonesboro (121 S. McDonough St. per the county directory; verify) by phone or email before closing; there is no charge for the question or the appeal.
