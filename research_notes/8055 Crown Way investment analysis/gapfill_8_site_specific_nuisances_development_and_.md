## Key Question 1: Where does the parcel sit relative to ATL's 2024/2029 65 DNL contours and the Noise Insulation Program (NIP) boundary?

### Takeaway
The official 2024/2029 Noise Exposure Map (NEM) report and the NIP information PDF are both hosted on atl.com, which returned HTTP 403 to every fetch attempt (two different user agents, two sessions), and the FAA's Federal Register acceptance notice contains no contour geography, so the parcel's contour position is still NOT verified from a primary source. What is verifiable: the FAA accepted the 2024 Existing and 2029 Future NEMs effective September 18, 2025; the NIP's published eligibility test is "inside the 65 DNL contour + eligible property type + no prior participation + built before April 10, 1985"; the house is 7.1 miles from the east end of the runway complex and the county GIS shows no airport-related overlay on the parcel. On those facts the house is very unlikely to be inside the 65 DNL contour (and therefore not NIP-eligible), but the buyer should confirm with the Department of Aviation before relying on it.

- Resale implication: no NIP-insulation benefit, but also no "inside the noise contour" disclosure stigma; the moderate third-party noise scores are most plausibly from Tara Blvd (0.3-0.5 mi) and overflight approaches rather than contour-level exposure.

## Key Question 4: What does the Clayton County Municode text actually say about short-term rentals and accessory dwellings in RS-110?

### Takeaway
The Municode text itself is still unreadable by automation: library.municode.com returned HTTP 403 and api.municode.com returned a Cloudflare challenge in this pass, as in earlier passes.
