## Key Question 2: Are there recorded covenants running with the lot, and could they restrict leasing?

### Takeaway
No declaration of covenants for Rosewood Estates is findable online and none is referenced by the county (zoning RS110 is the only land-use control the GIS shows). Even if the 1968 developer recorded covenants, Georgia's O.C.G.A. § 44-5-60(b) limits pre-July-1993 restrictive covenants in zoned jurisdictions to 20 years, so 1968 covenants would have expired in 1988 unless the covenants themselves contained a self-renewal clause (Sweeney, 2004); there is no HOA and no evidence of any renewal, so a developer-era leasing restriction is very unlikely to bind the buyer.

### Cited Findings
- O.C.G.A. § 44-5-60(b): covenants restricting land to certain uses may not run longer than 20 years in municipalities or counties with zoning laws; subsection (d) (added 1993) makes covenants in planned subdivisions of 15 or more lots renew automatically for further 20-year periods unless 51% of lot owners vote to terminate — [LegiScan text of 2011 SB 340 (quoting current § 44-5-60)](https://www.legiscan.com/GA/text/SB340/2011); [FindLaw case summary](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/ga-court-of-appeals/1138223.html)
- Lueder, Larkin & Hunter survey of Georgia covenant-duration law: the 1993 amendment "took effect July 1, 1993"; Appalachee Enterprises, Inc. v. Walker, 266 Ga. 35 (1995) held the 1993 amendment does not apply retroactively to covenants recorded before July 1, 1993; Bickford v. Yancey Development Co., 276 Ga. 814 (2003): the automatic-renewal provision "applies only to covenants established after the revision became effective on July 1, 1993" and pre-1993 restrictive covenants "are governed by Code Section 44-5-60 and thus are deemed unenforceable after a period of 20 years"; Canterbury Forest Association v. Collins, 243 Ga. App. 425 (2000): pre-1993 covenants without a renewal clause cannot be amended to add one; Sweeney v. The Landings Association, 277 Ga. 761 (2004): a renewal clause written into 1972 covenants was "valid and enforceable under contract law"; Arbor Station HOA v. Dorman, 255 Ga. App. 866 (2002): affirmative covenants (duty to pay assessments) are not restrictive covenants and do not expire under § 44-5-60 — [Lueder Law, "Duration of Covenants: A Survey of Georgia Law"](https://www.luederlaw.com/duration-of-covenants-a-survey-of-georgia-law/)
- Parcel zoning RS110 and no HOA/overlay other than the "ROSEWOOD ESTATES" neighborhood code on the county GIS (prior notes, queried 2026-10-18) — [Clayton County GIS parcel layer](https://gis.claytoncountyga.gov/server/rest/services/TaxAssessor/Parcels/MapServer/0)

## Key Question 3: Which licensed contractors did the recent roof, electrical-panel and window work, and do the warranties transfer?

### Takeaway
No contractor can be identified from public records because no permit of any kind was recorded for this parcel in Clayton County's 2016-2025 permit exports (gapfill_2), and Georgia does not license roofers or window installers at all (they are exempt "specialty contractors"); only the panel work legally required a Georgia-licensed electrical contractor (Class I or II). The contractor names, dates and transferable warranties therefore exist only in the seller's paperwork: the buyer must ask the listing agent (Burnie King, KW Signature Partners) for invoices, the shingle manufacturer's registered warranty, and the electrician's license number, then verify the electrician at the Secretary of State and, for the roof, file the manufacturer's transfer within one year of closing (GAF: once, in writing, within one year; same coverage if within 20 years of installation).

### Inferences
- Because roofers and window installers are unlicensed in Georgia, there is no state registry to search for "who did the roof"; the only verifiable party is the electrician (SOS licensee search by name or license number, free). A panel replacement without a permit also means no county inspection record; the buyer should (a) demand the electrician's name and license number and (b) have the panel inspected by a licensed electrician during due diligence, or require the seller to obtain an after-the-fact electrical permit and inspection as a closing condition.
- Manufacturer shingle warranties (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed) only exist if the installer registered the job; the buyer should ask for the registration number or certificate and the installation invoice (which also proves the "less than five years old" claim). Extended "System Plus / Golden Pledge" warranties require a GAF-certified contractor, and that certification would identify a legitimate company.
- Estate sellers (two out-of-state executors) often do not have the decedent's invoices; if none surface, the listing's upgrade claims rest solely on visual inspection. Price the roof as "unknown age, visually recent" and the panel as "unpermitted" until documents are produced.
- Exact buyer request list: (1) to listing agent: roof invoice/contract and manufacturer warranty certificate; window invoice and window manufacturer (vinyl window warranties are usually lifetime to original owner, transferable once, e.g. within 30-90 days of sale, check the brand's terms); electrical panel invoice with the contractor's Georgia license number; (2) to Georgia SOS Licensee Search: verify the electrician's license status (free); (3) to Clayton County Building Permits (770-477-3569 or EnerGov CSS in a browser): confirm no permits/inspections and ask whether a homeowner affidavit permit can be pulled retroactively; (4) within one year after closing: file the shingle warranty transfer with the manufacturer.
