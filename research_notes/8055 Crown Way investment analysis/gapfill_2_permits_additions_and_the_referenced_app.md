## Key Question 3: Can the listing agent supply the appraisal behind "2,117 sq ft per appraisal" (date, purpose, value conclusion), and how will lenders treat the unpermitted space?

### Takeaway
The appraisal is not a public record and nothing online identifies its date, purpose or value; the MLS "Living Area Source: Appraiser" and the 2,117 figure (2 sq ft under the county's 2,119) show that a licensed appraiser measured the house including the addition. The buyer must ask the listing agent (Burnie King, Keller Williams Realty Signature Partners, 678-631-1700) for a copy; if the seller ordered it (pre-listing or estate), she can release it, but if a lender ordered it the appraiser cannot give it to a third party. For resale, Fannie Mae's current Selling Guide (B4-1.3-05, dated 06/04/2025) does not forbid lending on unpermitted additions; it requires the appraiser to comment on quality and market impact and to measure under ANSI Z765-2021, while FHA's position (as relayed by HUD field guidance) is that an addition may not be excluded from value solely for lack of permits, though individual lenders may impose stricter overlays.

### Inferences
- The 2,117 vs 2,119 match means the appraiser counted the 544-sq-ft frame addition as gross living area; the appraisal therefore already treats the fourth-bedroom suite as finished, interior-accessible space, which is the best available evidence that it is heated, connected to the house and built to a comparable standard (the three FHA tests). The buyer should confirm by walking from the main house into the suite without going outside.
- Because the appraisal's date, client and purpose are unknown, its value conclusion cannot be used as a price anchor yet. Three possibilities, in order of likelihood for a 20-year senior owner-occupant listing: (a) a pre-listing appraisal ordered by the seller or her family/attorney-in-fact in 2026 (freely shareable; ask for the full report, not just page 1); (b) an estate/date-of-death appraisal if the owner is deceased (shareable by the executor; ask also for Letters Testamentary); (c) a lender appraisal from a refinance or reverse mortgage (the appraiser is bound by USPAP confidentiality to the lender and can share only with the client's consent). The request to the agent should ask for the effective date, the intended use, the appraiser's name and Georgia license number, the sketch page (which will show the addition's dimensions and whether it was measured per ANSI) and the opinion of value.
- For the exit: a conventional buyer's appraiser will count the suite as GLA under ANSI but must note the missing permit and its effect on value; the detached garage room, with no interior access, will be reported as an ancillary structure with little or no GLA credit; the covered patio is a site improvement. The realistic financing risk is lender overlays (some lenders assign unpermitted space zero value) rather than an outright block, so the investor should obtain after-the-fact permits or at least a licensed-contractor inspection letter for the panel and addition before relisting, and should underwrite the resale on 1,575 sq ft plus a discounted addition if a conservative case is wanted.
- Insurance: insurers generally underwrite on the full replacement cost of all structures; an unpermitted panel upgrade is the item most likely to trigger an insurer's electrical-inspection condition. (General industry practice; no Georgia-specific source captured.)
