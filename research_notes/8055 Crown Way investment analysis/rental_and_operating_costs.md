### Inferences
- Computed rental vacancy rate from ACS 2024 1-year (vacant-for-rent ÷ [renter-occupied + for-rent + rented-not-occupied]) = 6,527 ÷ 50,414 = **12.9%**, well above the 9% third-party figure; 1-year ACS margins are wide, so treat 9–13% as the plausible county range. For a renovated single-family house (21-day lease-up) an 8% vacancy-and-credit-loss allowance is defensible; 5% is aggressive given Clayton's eviction profile.
- Affordability screen: at the common 3× rent-to-income rule, a $2,050 rent needs about $73,800 household income, roughly 1.7× the county renter median ($42,403). The qualified tenant pool is therefore thin for the top of the rent range; voucher households (payment standard up to $2,552) and two-earner families are the natural market.
- The "150 per week" figure (about 7,800/yr) is far below the 2023 count (19,913) and the LSC cumulative pace (about 16,000/yr between March 2024 and mid-2026); the Civic Circle figure likely understates. Plan on filing-to-writ timelines being slower in Clayton than neighboring counties.
- Budget an eviction cycle in reserves: $50 filing fee plus 1–3 months of lost rent and turnover (about $2,500–$7,000 per event at $2,050 rent) given understaffed court calendars.
