### Inferences
- **JHA's 4BR payment standard for 30238 is unverified and should be modeled as a range, not a point**: $2,088 (90%), $2,320 (100%) or $2,552 (110%) of the FY2027 SAFMR. The earlier notes' Section 8 thesis implicitly assumed 110%; the only Georgia evidence for 110% is DCA's 2024 table ($2,563 for 30238 equals $2,330 x 1.10, i.e. 110% of what must have been the FY2024 SAFMR; the FY2024 SAFMR file was not fetched to confirm), and DCA itself proposed moving to 90% in June 2026. If JHA has followed the same fiscal pressure, the 4BR standard could be as low as $2,088, below the $2,115-$2,140 private asking rents.
- **Timing of any re-basing**: the FY2027 SAFMRs took effect 2026-10-01 and are 4.1% lower than FY2026 for 4BR in 30238. Whether JHA has already lowered its schedule, is holding at the FY2026-based level for existing contracts, or will adjust on a January 1 cycle is unknown; a lease signed before any decrease is typically protected until the family's later reexamination, so a landlord who signs a HAP contract this autumn at a standard based on $2,420 would keep the higher figure for at least the first year (this timing rule is from 24 CFR 982.505 as generally understood; the regulation text could not be fetched and should be confirmed with JHA).
- **Rent reasonableness caps the voucher upside at market, not at the payment standard.** Even a $2,552 standard does not let the owner charge $2,552: JHA must find the contract rent reasonable against comparable unassisted 4BR houses, which Question 1 puts at roughly $1,950-$2,100 achieved and $2,115-$2,140 asked. A realistic approved contract rent for a renovated subject is therefore about $2,050-$2,150, perhaps $2,200 with documented superior condition (new roof, HVAC, four-sided brick, detached garage). The practical benefit of a voucher tenant is payment reliability and the ability to hold the top of the market range, not a premium above it.
- The tenant's share also matters: at initial lease-up the family's share may not exceed 40% of adjusted income, and the "gross rent" (contract rent plus JHA's utility allowance for a tenant-paid gas-heat, electric-cooled single-family house) is what is compared with the payment standard. With a utility allowance of a few hundred dollars a month (JHA's schedule not found), a $2,150 contract rent plus allowance would exceed a $2,088 or $2,320 standard and push the family's share up, narrowing the pool of eligible voucher families; at a $2,552 standard it would fit comfortably.
- JHA's waiting list has been closed since April 2021 (1,895 vouchers), so the voucher-holder pool is existing holders moving between units, not new issuances; demand for 4-bedroom units is real but the owner must still find a holder whose voucher size is 4BR (or 3BR with a larger standard not applicable) and who can afford the tenant share.
