At TPCi events, the open team list shows species, ability, item, moves and Stat Alignment. Timers are 90 s Team Preview, 45 s per move, 7 min player time and 20 min game time. — (Victory Road regulations page, victoryroad.pro/champions-regulations/, acc. 2026-09-28, T2)

All four Worlds 2026 semifinalists registered **two** Mega Stones — (Limitless VGC Worlds team lists, standings.limitlessvgc.com/0036/standings, acc. 2026-09-28, T2).
- Published Champions spreads are written in Stat Points, e.g. `Jolly:2/32/0/0/0/32` (HP/Atk/Def/SpA/SpD/Spe). In Smogon's August M-B file, 1,581 of 1,631 listed spreads sum to exactly 66, none sum to more, and no single stat goes above 32. — (Smogon usage stats, moveset file, smogon.com/stats/2026-08/moveset/gen9championsvgc2026regmb-1760.txt, pub. 2026-09-01, T2; Scout count) The rule itself belongs in `knowledge/mechanics.md`.

## 1. What changed from M-B, and what Worlds 2026 showed

1. **New roster.** The official announcement says **24 Pokémon** are newly available. The in-game roster notice lists 29 entries because it counts forms separately (both Toxtricity forms, both Indeedee, four Squawkabilly, and Persian plus Alolan Persian). The additions include Rillaboom, Salamence, Indeedee (M and F), Golisopod, Baxcalibur, Cinderace, Inteleon, Pawmot, Sirfetch'd, Grapploct, Mabosstiff, Toxtricity and Arboliva. So far only Rillaboom, Salamence, Indeedee and Golisopod show up in official results (§2). — (Pokemon.com M-C article, pub. 2026-09-02, T1; Pokémon HOME "Regular Roster M-C", news.pokemon-home.com/en/page/821.html, pub. 2026-09-09, T1)
2. **Six new Megas**: Mega Salamence, Mega Golisopod and Mega Baxcalibur, plus three "Z Mega Evolutions": Mega Absol Z, Mega Garchomp Z and Mega Lucario Z. Everything Mega-capable in M-B stays legal. — (Pokemon.com M-C article, T1; Victory Road regulations page, T2)
3. **Version 1.2.0** (released Sep 8, maintenance ended Sep 9 06:00 UTC):
   - Archaludon loses Mirror Coat and Metal Burst, and Politoed loses Pound. Slash becomes usable.
   - Wish and Strength Sap PP go from 12 to 8.
   - New in-battle "View Log" (history of every action this battle) and "Selection Support" (indicators on the selection screen). — (Pokémon HOME maintenance notice, news.pokemon-home.com/en/page/817.html, pub. 2026-09-09, T1)

"How it wins" lines are MY READ unless marked PLAYERS. They are built on the documented moves and abilities above.

### A. Rillaboom + Mega Raichu Y Grassy offense
Core: Rillaboom, Mega Raichu Y, Gholdengo, Hisuian Arcanine, plus Mega Staraptor, Sylveon, or Mega Garchomp Z with Volcarona.

Example teams (T1/T2):
- **Eric Rios, Frankfurt 1st (17-0):** Gholdengo, Volcarona (Rage Powder/Tailwind), Mega Garchomp Z, Incineroar, Rillaboom, Mega Raichu Y — (RK9 team list, Frankfurt roster, T1)
- **Conner Pietrusinski, Baltimore 7th:** H-Arcanine, Rillaboom, Gholdengo, Mega Staraptor, Mega Raichu Y, Sylveon — (Limitless /0037/player/0366/teamlist, T2)
- **Lorenzo Arce, Baltimore 5th:** Milotic, Mega Raichu Y, Ceruledge, Mega Staraptor, Gholdengo, Rillaboom — (Limitless, T2)
- **Esa Ishaque, Baltimore 6th:** adds Mega Froslass as a second Mega — (Limitless, T2)
- Pikalytics' 4-core Arcanine-Hisui / Gholdengo / Raichu-Mega-Y / Rillaboom appears on 450 teams (5.1%) — (Pikalytics tournaments, T2)

How it wins:
- Two Fake Out users (Rillaboom 98%, Mega Raichu Y 75%) buy free turns.
- Mega Raichu Y's No Guard Zap Cannon is guaranteed paralysis, which works as speed control.
- Gholdengo sets up Nasty Plot behind Fake Out pressure and spreads Make It Rain.
- Grassy Glide and Extreme Speed clean up with priority.

### B. Mega Salamence Tailwind offense
Core: Mega Salamence with Fake Out partners (Rillaboom, Sneasler, Incineroar).

- DATA: the Rillaboom / Mega Salamence / Sneasler trio is the most common 3-core (14.2%) — (Pikalytics tournaments, T2).
- Example: **Haruki Sato, Brisbane 2nd:** Mega Floette, Kingambit, Basculegion, Rillaboom, Mega Salamence (Modest; Flamethrower/Protect/Tailwind/Hyper Voice), Sneasler (Poison Touch Sash) — (RK9 Brisbane roster, T1)

How it wins:
- Intimidate on entry, Tailwind on turn 1 under Fake Out cover, then Aerilate Hyper Voice spread.
- Draco Meteor or Flamethrower cover the Steel and Ice checks.

DATA caution: Salamence converted slightly worse into Phase 2 than its field share (37.3% → 31.6%) and had a 48.8% win rate at Baltimore.

### C. Psychic Terrain: Indeedee + Psychic Seed Unburden Sneasler (sand, Mega Gardevoir, or Trick Room variants)
Example teams:
- **Joseph Ugarte, Baltimore 1st** — (Limitless /0037/player/0991/teamlist, T2):
  - Mega Salamence, Mega Tyranitar, Excadrill (Sand Rush, Sash)
  - Choice Scarf Indeedee-M (Expanding Force/Mystical Fire/Trick)
  - Corviknight (Psychic Seed; Brave Bird/Power Trip/Bulk Up/Roost)
  - Sneasler (White Herb; Coaching)
- **Blaik Thompson, Baltimore 4th:** Mega Salamence, Mega Metagross (Steel Roller), H-Arcanine, Choice Scarf Indeedee-M (Trick Room/Imprison), Milotic, Psychic Seed Sneasler — (Limitless, T2)
- **Wolfe Glick, Baltimore 15th:** Mega Gardevoir + Indeedee-F + Mega Salamence + Gholdengo + Incineroar + Psychic Seed Sneasler — (Limitless, T2)
- **Yvar Vlieger, Frankfurt 3rd:** Mega Charizard Y, Kingambit, **Mega Meowstic-F**, Psychic Seed Sneasler, Focus Sash Indeedee-M (Trick Room), Kommo-o — (RK9 Frankfurt roster, T1)

How it wins:
- Psychic Terrain boosts Expanding Force into a spread move (Showdown move data, T2).
- The Psychic Seed triggers Unburden, so Sneasler outspeeds without Tailwind.
- The terrain also contests Rillaboom's Grassy Terrain.
- Psychic Terrain blocking priority is Scarlet/Violet behavior. UNVERIFIED in Champions; see §7.

### D. Rain and dual weather ("screens rain")
Core: Pelipper or Politoed, Archaludon, and Mega Golisopod or Mega Swampert, often with Mega Charizard Y + Venusaur as a sun mode and Grimmsnarl screens.

Example teams:
- **Kiran Singh, Brisbane 1st:** Archaludon, Pelipper, Grimmsnarl, Mega Charizard Y, Mega Garchomp Z, Venusaur — (RK9 Brisbane roster, T1)
- **Aditya Subramanian, Baltimore 2nd:** Mega Golisopod, Mega Charizard Y, Politoed, Archaludon, Grimmsnarl, Farigiraf — (Limitless, T2)
- **Daniel Quek, Brisbane 8th** — (RK9, T1)
- **Joshua Sutherland-Smith, Brisbane 4th:** rain + Mega Floette — (RK9, T1)
- At Worlds, the same core won the Seniors division (Vikram Thiagarajan: Pelipper, Mega Swampert, Archaludon, Mega Charizard Y, Venusaur, Grimmsnarl) — (Limitless Worlds Seniors standings, standings.limitlessvgc.com/0036/SR/standings, T2)

How it wins:
- Electro Shot fires without a charge turn in rain (Showdown move data, T2).
- Stamina Archaludon plus Light Clay screens are hard to remove.
- Pelipper Tailwind (85%) and Wide Guard (65%) cover the matchup against spread moves.
- Mega Charizard Y's sun flips the weather into a second mode against opposing rain or Water types.

### E. Mega Gengar Perish trap
Core: Mega Gengar, Politoed, Rillaboom or Incineroar, Vivillon or Kommo-o.

Examples: **Brady Smith, Baltimore 3rd** and **Billy Helm, Baltimore 10th** (Limitless, T2); **Anthony Liuzzo, Frankfurt 5th** (RK9, T1). All three use Mega Gengar with Perish Song.

- DATA: Mega Gengar's Showdown-data ability is Shadow Tag. Perish Song appears on 80% of Mega Gengar and 59% of Politoed — (Showdown pokedex data, T2; Pikalytics tournaments, T2).
- PLAYERS: the World Champion rated rain Perish as a "quite unfavorable" matchup for his M-B team — (natsumewato note, T3).

How it wins: Shadow Tag traps the target, then Perish Song plus Protect/Encore cycling and Vivillon sleep or redirection run down the opponent's four.

## 5. Holes in the meta (hand-off to the Lab; MY READ, built on cited data)

1. **Fast special Dragon vs thinning Fairy coverage.** Mega Garchomp Z (pure Dragon, Levitate, Speed 151) won two of the three M-C Regionals. Meanwhile the main Fairy spread and priority users fell hard from Worlds to Baltimore: Floette 27.6→11.9% and Sylveon 24.8→11.8% (Limitless, T2). The cheapest counters on paper are Pixilate **Quick Attack** (Fairy priority, on 82% of Sylveon), Fairy Aura Dazzling Gleam, Ice Beam or Icy Wind Milotic (58% / 48%), and Choice Scarf Garchomp (253 max speed, above Mega Garchomp Z's 223). **Lab ask:** is a Sylveon or Floette + Scarf Garchomp shell underpriced right now?
2. **Mega Gengar Perish trap.** 4.5% usage at Baltimore, a 55.0% win rate, and three top 8s across two M-C Regionals (Baltimore 3rd and 10th; Frankfurt 5th). The World Champion called it a bad matchup for the Worlds-winning archetype (note, T3). **Lab ask:** measure how many top-20 cores have an answer to Shadow Tag plus Perish Song. Ghost types escaping trapping is Scarlet/Violet behavior, so confirm it in Champions first.
3. **Screens rain won Brisbane.** Screen-breaking is rare among common moves. Across the ~30 most-used Pokémon's displayed move lists, the only screen breaker is Mega Metagross's Psychic Fangs (95% of Mega Metagross, which is on about 5% of teams). Brick Break and Defog don't appear (Pikalytics tournaments, T2). Psychic Fangs and Brick Break destroy screens in Showdown's move data (T2). **Lab ask:** test a screen-breaking slot against Grimmsnarl (Light Clay 94%) rain.
4. **Fake Out saturation.** Rillaboom (98%), Incineroar (99%), Mega Raichu Y (75%) and Sneasler (52%) carry Fake Out, and 14 of 24 M-C top 8s had Rillaboom. Possible answers: Psychic Terrain priority-blocking, if Champions keeps the Scarlet/Violet rule, and Quick Guard, seen on Sash Sneasler at Baltimore (Basham, Tang). **Lab ask:** anti-Fake-Out leads for the Trainer's eventual team.
5. **Sleep is answered mostly by accident.** Sleep Powder (Vivillon, Venusaur) and Hypnosis (30% of Milotic) appear, but Lum Berry and Safety Goggles are absent from every top-20 Pokémon's displayed item list (Pikalytics tournaments, T2). The main protection is incidental Grass typing (Rillaboom 54%), since Grass types are immune to powder in Scarlet/Violet; confirm in Champions. **Lab ask:** is a sleep lead punishing in Ranked, where there's no open team list?
