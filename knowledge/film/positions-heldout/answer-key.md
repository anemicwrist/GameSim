# Held-out answer key: Singles supplement

**Evaluation only (README 4.6).** These positions are never used in lessons or drills, and the Coach must not open
this folder. Use them only in Phase 8 to test whether film study is working: give the Trainer (or an agent) the
position file, collect the answer, then grade it against this key.

- Batch: Singles supplement, batch 1 (2026-09-28). 2 of this batch's 10 positions are held out.
- Source: non-official Pokémon Showdown ladder games ([Gen 9 Champions] BSS Reg M-C), not in-game ranked. "What the
  player did" is ladder play, not pro play, so the recommended answer can differ from it.
- **Contamination note:** each source match file's Turn Log records these turns as `[log]` lines, which is unavoidable
  because every turn is logged. The match files' Key Decisions, Turning Points and Lessons deliberately don't analyze
  these two turns. When grading, check whether the answerer quotes the match file's turn log for the outcome.

## ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2688548425-t6 (Ghostofcolress, p2, difficulty 2)

**What Ghostofcolress did [log]:** switched Samurott-Hisui out to Cinderace.

**What happened [log]:** sky2132 Mega Evolved Golisopod (Bug/Steel) and used Leech Life, which Cinderace resisted
(100% → 75%); Golisopod drained to 95%. On T7 Cinderace (faster) used High Jump Kick (Golisopod → 45%), and Drill Run
took Cinderace to 33%. On T8 Pyro Ball (4x) KO'd Golisopod. Samurott-Hisui, still at 100% with its Focus Sash, came
back on T9 and won the endgame: the Sash held against Electro Shot on T10, Sacred Sword hit on T10, and Aqua Jet
finished Archaludon on T11.

**Recommended answer: switch to Cinderace.** The actual play was right.

**Analysis (my read):**
1. **Golisopod threatens Samurott-Hisui.** Its Bug STAB is 2x on Samurott's Dark typing, and T6 is Golisopod's first
   turn out, so First Impression (+2 priority) is possible. If Samurott stays, it likely spends its Sash (or dies) for
   one Ceaseless Edge and a third Spikes layer. Its other options are weak here: Sacred Sword is resisted by a Bug/Water
   Golisopod and only neutral on Mega Golisopod, and Aqua Jet is weak.
2. **Cinderace is the ideal switch-in.** It resists Bug, it's far faster (base Speed 119 against 40), and Mega
   Evolution turns Golisopod Bug/Steel, which is **4x weak to Fire**. Even halved by rain, Pyro Ball stays 2x
   effective. Against a Golisopod that doesn't Mega Evolve (Bug/Water), Pyro Ball is only neutral, so the switch is
   best when you expect the Mega, which is the likely play since sky2132 hasn't used it yet.
3. **Keep the Sash for the endgame.** A full-HP Samurott-Hisui with Focus Sash and Aqua Jet is the closer against
   Archaludon (Sacred Sword is 2x on Steel/Dragon). That's exactly how the game ended.
4. **A third Spikes layer isn't worth the Sash.** Three layers take 1/4 on every switch-in, which is strong, but paying
   Samurott's Sash (and probably Samurott itself) for it leaves Archaludon unchecked.

**Grading:**
- **Full credit:** switch to Cinderace, with at least two of these reasons: (a) Cinderace resists Bug and outspeeds;
  (b) Mega Golisopod becomes Bug/Steel, so Fire is 4x; (c) keeping Samurott's Focus Sash for the endgame against
  Archaludon.
- **Partial credit:** stay in and Ceaseless Edge for a third Spikes layer, *if* the answer weighs the risk to the Sash
  and to Samurott.
- **No credit:** Sacred Sword or Aqua Jet as the main plan, or any reasoning that treats Golisopod as Bug/Water after it
  Mega Evolves.

## ss-gen9championsbssregmc-2688811994-t3 (Ch3t1x, p1, difficulty 2)

**What Ch3t1x did [log]:** Dragon Dance, without Mega Evolving.

**What happened [log]:** Gyarados moved first (+1 Atk, +1 Spe). Samurott-Hisui used Ceaseless Edge (Gyarados → 59%,
Spikes ×2). On T4 Gyarados Mega Evolved and KO'd Samurott with Waterfall. On T5 a +1 Waterfall (critical hit) KO'd
Ninetales-Alola, and on T6 a +1 Waterfall took Mega Charizard X to 28%. Ch3t1x won on T8.

**Recommended answer: Mega Evolve and Dragon Dance.**

**Analysis (my read):**
1. **A 1% Samurott gets at most one more action.** No single hit from a −1 Atk Samurott KOs a 100% Gyarados, so a setup
   turn costs HP, not the game.
2. **Mega Evolution resolves at the start of the turn, before any move.** The log shows this on T4 and T6. Samurott
   chose its move against a Water/Flying Gyarados, and Ceaseless Edge is the natural click (neutral STAB plus a Spikes
   layer). Against a Water/Dark Mega Gyarados that Dark move is **resisted**, and Mega Gyarados has more Defense (base
   109 against 79), so the hit would do well under half of the 41% it did. There's no reason to hold the Mega:
   Gyaradosite is the only Mega Stone among the three, and Ch3t1x Mega Evolved the next turn anyway.
3. **Dragon Dance without the Mega (the actual play) is second best.** It paid 41% and a second Spikes layer for
   +1/+1, and the boost paid off.
4. **Waterfall now is the safe line.** Gyarados turned out to be faster, so Samurott would have died before acting
   unless it used Aqua Jet (priority, resisted, from −1 Atk, so little damage). But Gyarados would then face
   Ninetales-Alola (Aurora Veil, snow) and Mega Charizard X unboosted.
5. **Caveat:** Mega Gyarados is weak to Fighting, and Samurott-Hisui often carries Sacred Sword (this player's did in
   game 2688548425). But the opponent picks its move before seeing the Mega, and Sacred Sword is resisted by a
   Water/Flying Gyarados, so it's an unlikely click this turn.

**Grading:**
- **Full credit:** Mega Evolve plus Dragon Dance, explaining that the Mega typing (Water/Dark) resists the Ceaseless
  Edge picked against the pre-Mega typing, and that there was no reason to delay the Mega.
- **Partial credit:** Dragon Dance without the Mega (the actual play), or Waterfall immediately, with sound reasoning
  (for example, that a 1% Samurott gets one action at most, or that Waterfall is the safest KO).
- **No credit:** switching to Garchomp (it takes Spikes and wastes a turn against a 1% target), or any line that
  expects the 1% Samurott to survive a hit.
