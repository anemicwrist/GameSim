## Lessons
- L1 — era: champions | mode: doubles-only | topic: priority stacking / double-targeting | basis: observed (report) + player plan.
  Two priority attacks on one target (Feint at +2 and Aqua Jet at +1) took down the opponent's Mega Charizard Y. The turn order and HP on that turn are Not available from permitted sources. Yamazaki built the team around this idea: every usual pick has a priority move, so two stacked priority hits KO "from unexpected range." — (pokemon.com Quick Attack, 2026-09-02, Tier 1); (Yamazaki report, https://note.com/rapid_mint7926/n/nf439490f62a6, 2026-09-06, Tier 3)
- L2 — era: champions | mode: singles-transferable | topic: team building (coverage vs speed trade-off) | basis: player's own post-event assessment.
  Onishi traded Charizard's Solar Beam for max-Speed Ancient Power to win mirrors. The resulting hole against Water types (Basculegion) is what he says hurt him in the deciding game. When you cut a coverage move, write down which opposing Pokémon you now can't beat, and check whether a finalist-level team carries it. — (Onishi report, https://note.com/cona_5757/n/n0b5081073f01, 2026-09-13, Tier 3)
- L3 — era: champions | mode: singles-transferable | topic: speed benchmarks / information | basis: player's own report (set-level; game unspecified).
  Onishi assumed he would win the Basculegion Speed contest and lost it (Yamazaki S123 against his roughly 119). Yamazaki had invested Speed specifically "to move first in the Basculegion mirror." Open team lists don't show stat points, so treat a Speed contest against an unknown spread as a risk, not a given. — (Onishi report, 2026-09-13, Tier 3); (Yamazaki report, 2026-09-06, Tier 3)
